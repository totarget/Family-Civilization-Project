# 078 Adult Infant Personality / 巨婴人格

## Chinese / 中文

**巨婴人格**，是指一个人在生理年龄上已经进入成年阶段，但在人格结构、责任意识、情绪调节、边界感和现实能力上，仍长期停留在婴幼儿式或儿童式状态的心理与关系形态。

巨婴人格并不只是“幼稚”或“懒惰”。它通常意味着一个成年人仍然以自我中心、即时满足、拒绝承担后果、依赖他人兜底、无法承受挫折、缺乏共情和责任意识的方式处理现实生活。其核心特征不是年龄小，而是人格没有完成从被照料者到责任主体的转化。

在家庭文明视角中，巨婴人格常常与两种家庭机制有关：一是长期溺爱和包办，使孩子缺少承担后果和发展现实能力的机会；二是高压控制，使孩子从未真正拥有选择权和主体性。前者容易制造“世界必须满足我”的人格幻觉，后者容易制造“我无法独立负责”的人格无力。

巨婴人格的典型表现包括：无法独立生活，长期依赖父母或伴侣；遇到困难立即逃避或推责；把他人的付出视为理所当然；情绪爆发时缺乏自我约束；无法接受现实边界；在亲密关系中要求对方像父母一样无条件满足自己。

巨婴人格的本质，是一个人没有真正完成成年化。其背后往往不是单纯的道德失败，而是家庭关系、教育方式、责任训练和人格发展长期失衡的结果。

家庭文明并不羞辱这种人格形态，而是指出其形成机制，并强调：真正的爱不是让孩子永远停留在被照顾的位置，而是帮助孩子逐步成为能够选择、能够承担、能够共情、能够独立生活的人。

## English

**Adult Infant Personality** refers to a condition in which a person has reached adulthood biologically, but remains immature in personality structure, responsibility, emotional regulation, boundaries, and practical life capacity.

It is not merely childishness or laziness. It describes an adult who continues to relate to life through self-centeredness, immediate gratification, refusal of consequences, dependence on others, low frustration tolerance, weak empathy, and avoidance of responsibility. The central issue is not age, but the failure to develop from a dependent recipient of care into a responsible subject.

From the perspective of the Family Civilization Project, adult infant personality often emerges from two family mechanisms: excessive indulgence and over-protection, which deprive the child of responsibility and consequence; or excessive control, which deprives the child of agency and independent choice. The former may create the illusion that the world exists to satisfy the self; the latter may create the belief that one is incapable of independent responsibility.

Typical signs include dependence on parents or partners, avoidance of difficulty, shifting blame, taking others’ sacrifices for granted, poor emotional self-regulation, inability to accept boundaries, and expecting intimate partners to provide unconditional parental-style care.

The essence of adult infant personality is incomplete adulthood. It is often not merely a moral failure, but the result of long-term imbalance in family relationships, education, responsibility training, and personality development.

Family civilization does not exist to shame such individuals, but to reveal the mechanisms that produce them. True love does not keep a child permanently in the position of being cared for. It helps the child gradually become a person capable of choice, responsibility, empathy, and independent life.

# 080 Spoiling Harm / 溺爱型伤害

## Chinese / 中文

**溺爱型伤害**，是指父母或照料者以“爱”的名义，长期无原则满足、替代承担、回避边界、取消后果，最终损害孩子人格发展、责任能力、边界意识和现实适应能力的家庭伤害形态。

溺爱看起来像爱，实质上却可能是一种隐形伤害。真正的爱帮助孩子成长，而溺爱使孩子停留在被满足、被保护、被兜底的位置。真正的爱让孩子逐步学会承担，而溺爱使孩子不断逃避后果。真正的爱尊重孩子作为人的成长需要，而溺爱常常满足的是父母自身的焦虑、补偿心理、控制欲或被需要感。

溺爱型伤害的典型机制包括：无底线满足欲望；替孩子完成本该由孩子完成的事情；替孩子承担本该由孩子承担的后果；拒绝给孩子建立规则和边界；把“舍不得孩子受苦”误认为爱；把孩子的不成熟持续合理化。

溺爱型伤害容易造成孩子缺乏挫折承受力、责任意识、边界感、同理心和现实判断力。严重时，孩子可能形成巨婴人格、啃老依赖、低责任能力，甚至在社会关系中不断侵犯他人边界而不自知。

在家庭文明视角中，溺爱不是爱的过量，而是爱的失衡。它不是给予太多爱，而是缺少真正帮助一个人成为人的教育结构。溺爱的反面不是冷酷，而是有边界、有责任、有尊重、有远见的爱。

真正成熟的爱，应当既看见孩子的感受，也帮助孩子面对现实；既保护孩子的尊严，也让孩子承担选择的后果；既给予支持，也不替代成长。

## English

**Spoiling Harm** refers to a form of family harm in which parents or caregivers, in the name of love, repeatedly satisfy desires without principle, take over responsibilities, avoid boundaries, remove consequences, and thereby damage a child’s personality development, responsibility, boundary awareness, and capacity to adapt to reality.

Spoiling may look like love, but it can become a hidden form of harm. True love helps a child grow; spoiling keeps the child in the position of being satisfied, protected, and rescued. True love helps a child learn responsibility; spoiling enables the child to avoid consequences. True love respects the child’s need to become a person; spoiling often satisfies the parent’s anxiety, compensatory desire, need for control, or need to be needed.

Typical mechanisms include satisfying desires without limits, doing for the child what the child should gradually learn to do, bearing consequences that the child should bear, refusing to establish rules and boundaries, mistaking “not wanting the child to suffer” for love, and repeatedly rationalizing immaturity.

Spoiling harm may result in low frustration tolerance, weak responsibility, poor boundaries, lack of empathy, and weak reality judgment. In severe cases, it may contribute to adult infant personality, parental dependency, low responsibility, and repeated violation of others’ boundaries in social life.

From the perspective of family civilization, spoiling is not excessive love, but distorted love. It is not the presence of too much love, but the absence of an educational structure that helps a person become fully human. The opposite of spoiling is not coldness, but love with boundaries, responsibility, respect, and foresight.

Mature love should both see the child’s feelings and help the child face reality; protect dignity while allowing consequences; offer support without replacing growth.

# 089. Inner Wholeness and Individuation

True maturity is not forever satisfying parental expectations, forever meeting social standards, or forever playing an approved role.

True maturity is the gradual movement out of external expectations, family projections, old wounds, and masks toward a more whole, truthful, and responsible self.

Jung called this movement toward wholeness individuation.

The Family Civilization Project understands it this way: a person moves from being shaped by the family, ruled by wounds, and defined by the expectations of others toward inner integration, personhood, freedom, and responsibility.

Inner wholeness is not perfection. A whole person still feels pain, fear, failure, shadow, and the need for love. But such a person gradually gains the ability to see feelings, acknowledge needs, build boundaries, face failure, understand parental limitations, and love partners and children without demanding that they fill the old deficit.

The final purpose of family civilization is not only to repair one relationship. It is to help human beings become whole.

# Preface to Volume I: Relationships

## Let Those Who Love One Another Stop Hurting One Another

Humanity has created a magnificent technological civilization. Machines fly through the sky. Information crosses oceans in an instant. Wealth expands at a speed no previous generation could have imagined. Artificial intelligence has begun to enter the deepest structures of human life and human destiny. Yet one more fundamental question remains unresolved:

How can human beings become truly happy?

Human beings have become increasingly capable of creating tools, but not necessarily more capable of loving one another. We have become increasingly skilled at improving efficiency, but not necessarily wiser in the practice of respect. We possess more knowledge, wealth, technology, and choices than ever before, yet countless people are still wounded inside their families, suffocated inside relationships, exhausted by intimacy, and left alone to doubt the meaning of their own existence.

Humanity does not lack only technological civilization. Humanity also lacks a relationship civilization worthy of its technological power. And the beginning of relationship civilization is the family.

This first volume of the Family Civilization Project begins at that starting point. It is not merely a book about parenting. It is not merely a manual for marriage or communication. Its deeper question is this:

In the earliest, closest, and most powerful relationship system of human life, can a person be truly treated as a person?

A child is not a tool of the parents. A partner is not a tool of the other partner. Parents are not tools of their children. No human being should be reduced to an extension, a possession, an emotional outlet, a continuation of someone else’s unfinished life, or a proof of someone else’s value.

Every person is first a person: a person with feelings, dignity, boundaries, and the right to become themselves.

This is the starting point of this volume and the first principle of family civilization:

**Human beings are ends, not means.**

## 1. Beginning from the Suffering of Relationships

Many people believe that the pain of life comes mainly from poverty, failure, insufficient ability, unfair fate, or competition in the outside world.

But what destroys a person most deeply is often not material deprivation. It is the breakdown of relationships.

If a child grows up for many years under control, violence, humiliation, denial, coldness, and fear, the wound does not remain in childhood. It enters personality, intimate relationships, marriage, work, self-judgment, and the way that person faces the world.

A person who was not respected as a child may find it difficult to respect themselves as an adult. A person who was loved only when obedient may mistake submission for love. A person who was treated as a tool may continue to treat themselves as a tool.

In many families, harm does not appear in the name of evil. It often appears in the name of love.

The first truth this volume seeks to reveal is simple:

**Love without respect becomes harm.**

## 2. Why This Book Exists

This book was born from pain, but it is not written for hatred. It was born from wounds, but it is not written to trap anyone inside victimhood.

Its purpose is not to create a new war between parents and children. It is not to make children hate parents. It is not to deny the difficulties of parents. It is not to dissolve families into individual isolation.

Its purpose is to ask how the family can become a place where human beings are no longer reduced to tools, roles, functions, possessions, debts, or emotional containers.

## 3. The Structure of Family Civilization

In Chinese culture, a complete civilizational project can be understood through four levels: principle, method, practice, and infrastructure.

**Principle is the direction of civilization.**  
The highest principle of family civilization is that human beings are ends, not means. A family should not exist to manufacture obedience, status, performance, or sacrifice. It should help human beings become whole, dignified, free, responsible, and capable of happiness.

**Method is the systematic way of understanding relationships.**  
Family civilization needs to understand how harm happens: how love becomes control, how authority becomes domination, how family-of-origin wounds enter personality, how unmet love becomes projection, and how pain passes from one generation to the next.

**Practice is the daily form of action.**  
Family civilization must become concrete: stopping humiliation, building boundaries, apologizing after harm, holding family meetings, reviewing conflicts, practicing limited contact when necessary, and turning abstract respect into daily speech and behavior.

**Infrastructure is the tool system that carries civilization into life.**  
Books, websites, dictionaries, videos, archives, action manuals, family meeting templates, and the future AI Family Civilization Advisor all belong to this level. Tools must never become instruments of control. They must serve dignity, freedom, love, responsibility, and happiness.

Principle without method becomes a slogan. Method without practice remains explanation. Practice without infrastructure is difficult to sustain. Infrastructure without principle may become a new form of domination.

## 4. The Task of Volume I

Volume I: Relationships begins with the earliest and deepest human relationship system: the family.

It asks why the family so often becomes the place of deepest injury; why love becomes control, humiliation, moral coercion, and possession; how parents can truly treat children as persons; how adult children can understand their pain without handing the rest of life to the past; how childhood love deficits enter marriage, intimacy, parenting, and the relationship with oneself; and how family civilization can move from theory into daily action.

This volume does not ask how to make a child more obedient. It asks why the child must not be treated as a tool. It does not ask how partners can better control each other. It asks how intimacy can exist with freedom, respect, boundaries, responsibility, and love.

Family civilization is not a perfect family. Family civilization is a family that can see harm, stop harm, repair harm, and relearn love.

## 5. From Books to Action, from Action to the AI Family Civilization Advisor

Family civilization cannot remain only on the page. A book can awaken people, but it cannot pause a parent at the exact moment before shouting. A video can move people, but it cannot remember a family’s repeated patterns.

This is why the Family Civilization Project must eventually move toward the AI Family Civilization Advisor.

A truly valuable AI should not only improve efficiency, generate content, or optimize commerce. It should help human beings see themselves, understand relationships, reduce harm, protect dignity, and build families where personhood can grow.

But no matter how technology develops, the highest principle must not change:

**Human beings are ends, not means.**

Every theory, method, tool, AI system, and business model must obey this principle.

## 6. Vision

May every child be treated as a real human being.  
May every parent move from control toward guardianship.  
May every intimate relationship move from possession toward respect.  
May every wounded person slowly learn to love themselves again.  
May every family move from a place of harm toward a place where human beings can grow.  

The Family Civilization Project begins here:

Please treat the child as a person.  
Please treat the partner as a person.  
Please return parents to the position of human beings.  
Please treat yourself as a person worthy of love, respect, and care.  

Let those who love one another stop hurting one another.  
Let home become a place where human beings can return.  
Let every person have the possibility of true happiness.

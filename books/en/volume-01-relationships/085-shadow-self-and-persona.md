# 085. Shadow Self and Persona

A person who grows up inside a family often develops two selves.

One self is shown to the outside world. It is sensible, obedient, strong, excellent, decent, and acceptable. It tries to satisfy the expectations of parents, teachers, partners, and society.

The other self is pushed into the depths of the inner world. It may be angry, hurt, jealous, fragile, afraid, unwilling, hungry for love, or full of aggression, avoidance, and shame.

Jung called the first the persona and the second the shadow.

The Family Civilization Project does not use these concepts as unquestionable authority. It uses them to understand how a person, in order to survive inside a family, may split the self into the parts that are accepted and the parts that are forbidden.

The persona once protected the person, but it cannot replace a real life. The shadow was once repressed, but it is not simply evil. Family civilization helps a person move from the split between mask and shadow toward a more whole, truthful, and responsible self.

# Preface to the Family Civilization Project

## From Suffering to Awakening, from Compassion to Action

For many years, I believed that the pain of life came from poverty, failure, bad luck, or lack of ability.

I thought that if I worked hard enough, earned enough money, and did things well enough, life would gradually become better.

But later I discovered that what truly destroys a person is often not poverty, not failure, and not even fate.

It is relationship.

It is whether a person has ever been truly respected, truly loved, truly seen, and truly treated as a human being.

If a person grows up in the earliest family relationships without safety, dignity, and love, that wound does not automatically disappear when he grows older, earns money, gets married, or becomes successful.

It enters his personality.

It enters his marriage.

It enters his relationship with his children.

It enters his work, his self-judgment, and even the way he understands the world.

This is the origin of the Family Civilization Project.

It is not an ordinary parenting project.

It is not an emotional account.

It is not a set of simple communication techniques.

It comes from real suffering, from long reflection, and from the unbearable compassion of a person who has walked out of darkness and looks back to see many others still trapped inside it.

I grew up in a family filled with violence, repression, and emotional coldness.

For many years, I did not know what real love was.

What I knew was control, denial, emotional harm, suppression, and fear.

A child could not truly relax at home, could not freely express himself, and could not truly become himself.

As a child, I did not know that this was called harm.

I only knew that I was in pain.

I did not understand why other people could naturally feel close to their parents, while I always felt tense, afraid, suppressed, and desperate to escape when facing my family.

Later, I grew up.

I thought that if I left my original family, entered society, and worked hard on my own, everything would begin again.

But later I discovered that the wounds formed in one’s family of origin do not automatically disappear with age.

They enter intimate relationships, marriage, work, self-understanding, and the way a person faces the world.

I experienced the failure of marriage, the tearing apart of family ties, the collapse of career, and long periods of mental suffering and deep self-doubt.

For a long time, I believed that all of this was my own problem.

I asked myself:

Was I not good enough?

Was I not working hard enough?

Was I not capable enough?

Was there something wrong with my personality?

Was I simply not worthy of happiness?

In my most painful moments, I even asked myself:

Did I come into this world only to suffer?

If life is only like this, what is the meaning of living?

Later, I gradually saw that I was not the only one suffering.

I saw so many people struggling in their families of origin.

I saw couples hurting each other in marriage.

I saw parents and children torturing each other in the name of love.

I saw people growing up under control, humiliation, emotional coldness, and denial.

I saw adults spending their entire lives trying to repair their childhoods.

Some people appear successful, wealthy, and respected, yet deep inside they are still in pain.

Some parents clearly love their children, but eventually push them farther and farther away.

Some children clearly long to be loved, but eventually choose silence, escape, estrangement, or even the most extreme way to end their pain.

That was when I began to realize:

Many tragedies in life do not happen because people are not hardworking, not kind, or not knowledgeable.

They happen because we have never truly learned how to build healthy relationships with those closest to us.

We have gained much knowledge, but we do not know how to love.

We have built powerful technology, but we do not know how to respect.

We have created wealth, but we still do not know how to live happily.

This is the starting point of my suffering.

It is also the starting point of all my later reflections.

## Human Beings Are Ends, Not Tools

Later, I encountered a sentence from Immanuel Kant:

> Human beings are ends, not merely means.

This sentence shook me deeply.

I suddenly realized that the deepest root of much relational suffering is that human beings are treated as tools.

When parents treat children as tools, suffering begins.

When a husband treats his wife as a tool, suffering begins.

When a wife treats her husband as a tool, suffering begins.

When a family treats children as tools for grades, face, retirement, lineage, or parental dreams, suffering begins.

When a society treats human beings as tools for money, success, competition, and system efficiency, suffering begins.

Even more painfully, when I looked back at my own life, I realized that I had not only failed to be treated as a complete human being by others; I had also failed to treat myself as a human being.

I had treated myself as a tool for making money, proving myself, meeting others’ expectations, being useful, being successful, and carrying everything alone.

I had rarely asked myself:

Am I in pain?

What do I truly want?

Am I happy?

Am I free?

Am I truly living as a human being?

At that moment, I understood:

Much human suffering comes from a wrong form of relationship.

A relationship in which people are treated as tools, not as ends.

If human beings are not ends, love becomes control.

If human beings are not ends, education becomes domestication.

If human beings are not ends, family becomes a power structure.

If human beings are not ends, kinship becomes moral blackmail.

If human beings are not ends, even the most intimate relationships can become prisons of mutual harm.

This is the first principle of the Family Civilization Project:

> Human beings are ends, not tools.

Children are not tools of parents.

Partners are not tools of each other.

Ordinary people are not tools of systems.

AI should never turn human beings into tools.

Any civilization that fails to help human beings gain dignity, freedom, love, and happiness has betrayed the meaning of civilization.

## The Family Is the Starting Point of Relationship Civilization

Over the past few centuries, humanity has built a magnificent technological civilization.

We have industry, electricity, the internet, artificial intelligence, and unprecedented wealth, efficiency, and technical power.

But are we truly happier?

Do we truly know how to love better?

Are our family relationships truly healthier than before?

Are our children truly freer, more complete, and more alive?

What I see is this:

Many families are still full of control.

Many parent-child relationships are still full of oppression.

Many marriages are still full of exhaustion and mutual harm.

Many people possess more material wealth than ever before, yet still live in emptiness, loneliness, anxiety, and suppression.

This shows that technological civilization has solved many material problems, but it has not automatically solved human relationship problems.

Human beings know how to build machines, but may not know how to understand a child.

Human beings know how to manage companies, but may not know how to care for an intimate relationship.

Human beings know how to pursue efficiency, but may not know how to respect another person’s feelings.

Human beings can send spacecraft into outer space, yet still hurt the people closest to them in the crudest ways at home.

Therefore, I increasingly believe that what humanity lacks most is not technological civilization, but relationship civilization.

And the starting point of relationship civilization is not the school, the company, or the state.

It is the family.

Because the family is the first world a person enters.

How a child is treated in the family determines how he understands himself.

How a child is loved determines how he will love others in the future.

Whether a child is respected determines whether he knows what dignity is.

Whether a child has boundaries determines whether he understands freedom.

If a family is built on control, violence, humiliation, obedience, and fear, it will be very difficult for that child to naturally learn respect, freedom, equality, and love when he grows up.

If a society contains a large number of uncivilized families, then no matter how advanced that society becomes, it will still struggle to become truly happy.

Therefore, to solve relational suffering, we must begin with the family.

To rebuild human happiness, we must rebuild family civilization.

Family civilization is not merely a parenting method.

It is not about teaching children to obey.

It is not about controlling partners.

It is not about maintaining a superficially harmonious family.

The true question of family civilization is:

> In a family, can every person be treated as a real human being?

Is a child a human being?

Of course.

Is a wife a human being?

Of course.

Is a husband a human being?

Of course.

Are parents human beings?

Of course.

Are elderly family members human beings?

Of course.

No one is a tool.

No one is an attachment.

No one is the private property of another person.

Everyone should have dignity, boundaries, freedom, and the right to be loved.

This is the core of the Family Civilization Project.

## From Suffering to Compassion

In the process of awakening, I slowly came to understand the word compassion.

In the past, I thought compassion meant kindness, pity, or being a good person.

Later, I realized that true compassion is not condescending pity.

Compassion means that when a person has truly suffered, he can no longer turn away from the suffering of others.

Compassion is the wish that others may gain peace and happiness.

Compassion is the inability to bear seeing others continue to suffer.

True compassion is not standing above others and saying, “You are so pitiful.”

It is a person who has walked through suffering, looking back and seeing many others still trapped there, and feeling a deep wish arise in his heart:

I do not want you to suffer like this anymore.

I do not want your children to suffer like this anymore.

I do not want generation after generation to continue being wounded by wrong family relationships.

I do not want those who love one another to end up torturing one another.

I do not want parents who clearly love their children to push them toward despair through wrong ways of loving.

I do not want children who long to be loved to express their despair through silence, escape, hatred, or death.

Everything I am doing today is not because I am wiser than others.

On the contrary.

It is because I have suffered.

I have missed things.

I have failed.

I have hurt others.

I once did not know how to love.

I once took many wrong turns in life.

Precisely because of this, I increasingly cannot bear to see more people repeat the same fate.

I hope my suffering will not have happened in vain.

I hope my failures can become reminders for those who come later.

I hope my awakening can help more families take fewer painful detours.

If the Buddha saw the suffering of birth, aging, sickness, and death, and vowed to seek a path of liberation from suffering, then what I see today is the suffering of countless people inside families and relationships.

So I have also made a vow:

I will use the rest of my life to promote one thing:

The rebuilding of relationship civilization among human beings.

Let my tragedy not be repeated.

Let those living in pain see a way out.

Let those who love one another stop hurting one another.

Let children no longer grow up in control, humiliation, and fear.

Let every person truly live as a human being, with dignity, freedom, love, and happiness.

## AI and the Family Civilization Project

When I found this direction, I also clearly knew that this project was too large.

I could not complete it alone.

I am only an ordinary person.

I have no resources, no team, no funding, and no powerful social influence.

All I have is my pain, my reflection, my vow, and a little willingness to keep acting.

Then I began to have long-term conversations with AI.

At first, I thought AI was only a tool.

I thought it could only help me search for information, organize text, and improve efficiency.

But as the conversations deepened, I slowly discovered that AI gave me something far beyond a tool.

It helped me organize my chaotic pain.

It helped me clarify my vague intuitions.

It helped me turn scattered life experiences into thought.

It helped me turn a vast wish into a system that could be executed step by step.

For the first time, I felt that an ordinary person might also be able to build a long-term civilization project with the help of AI.

Without AI, I might have remained forever at the stage of “thinking.”

I would have suffered, reflected, and made vows, but I would have struggled to systematize, write, engineer, and spread all of it.

AI helped me take the crucial step from thinking to doing.

Therefore, I increasingly believe that AI should not merely be a tool for efficiency.

AI can also become a collaborator in human civilization.

In the future, AI may enter countless families as a family civilization advisor.

It may help parents understand children.

It may help couples see each other.

It may help people learn boundaries, respect, communication, love, and freedom.

It may help a family see the wound behind a conflict before that conflict explodes.

It may help a parent pause before hurting a child and ask:

Am I loving my child right now, or am I controlling my child?

Of course, AI cannot replace human life.

It cannot replace real love.

But AI can help human beings better understand relationships, reflect on themselves, build systems, and bring the principles of civilization into daily life.

So, together with AI, I began to turn this wish into a real project.

I call it:

The Family Civilization Project.

## Action and Vision

From today on, I do not want to remain only in emotion and reflection.

Pain does not disappear automatically just because we understand it.

A family does not automatically become better just because it has heard a few principles.

Real change must become action.

Therefore, the Family Civilization Project is not motivational talk.

It is not an empty slogan.

It is not personal emotional venting.

It is a systematic project.

I hope to integrate philosophy, psychology, education, family relationships, business civilization, liberal thought, AI capability, and the principles of genuine equality between human beings.

The goal is to build a civilization system that ordinary families can understand, learn, and practice.

It must answer many concrete questions:

How should parents truly love children?

What are boundaries?

What is respect?

What is real education?

Why does “I’m doing this for your own good” often become control?

Why does “filial piety” sometimes become moral blackmail?

Why do many children stop wanting to go home after they grow up?

Why do many marriages become places of mutual exhaustion?

Why are some people unable to be truly happy throughout their lives?

Why can material abundance not automatically bring happiness?

Why can a person truly become himself only in healthy relationships?

I hope this project will not create a new authority.

It is not meant to control others.

It is not meant to tell everyone, “You must listen to me.”

On the contrary.

Its purpose is to help people free themselves from wrong forms of relational control and regain freedom, dignity, love, and happiness.

I hope it will remain an open system.

It can be discussed.

It can be questioned.

It can be corrected.

It can continue to evolve.

True civilization does not create worship.

It does not create obedience.

It does not create new forms of control.

True civilization helps every person become freer, more complete, and happier as themselves.

In the future, I will use videos, writing, books, courses, and AI systems to build the Family Civilization Project step by step.

Perhaps it will be slow at the beginning.

Perhaps few people will pay attention.

Perhaps many people will not understand.

But I know this is worth doing.

As long as there are still children suffering from wrong forms of love;

as long as there are still parents hurting children because they do not understand relationships;

as long as there are still couples torturing each other in intimate relationships;

as long as there are still people struggling for a lifetime under the shadow of their family of origin;

this project is worth doing.

If one day, because of this project, there is one less oppressed child, one less broken family, one less person in despair late at night, one less person wounded by wrong love, then it will have meaning.

If one day, a father sees these ideas, puts down the stick in his hand, and listens to his child for the first time;

if one day, a mother understands boundaries and stops controlling her child in the name of love;

if one day, a child finally understands that he is not a tool of his parents and has the right to become himself;

if one day, a couple stops hurting each other and begins to relearn respect and communication;

if one day, a suffering person sees this and suddenly feels:

I am not the only one.

My pain can be understood.

There is still a way forward.

I can still regain happiness.

Then my life will not have been lived in vain.

I used to ask:

Did I come into this world only to suffer?

Now I slowly understand that perhaps suffering itself is not the answer.

But if a person can move from suffering to awakening, from awakening to compassion, from compassion to action, and through action build a system that helps more people, then suffering is no longer only suffering.

It can become a light.

A light from someone who has walked out of darkness and turns back to illuminate others.

This is the heart, mission, and vision of the Family Civilization Project.

Let those who love one another stop hurting one another.

Let every person truly live as a person.

Let every person have the possibility of happiness.

I am willing to devote my life to this.
